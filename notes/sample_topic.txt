Photosynthesis: How Plants Make Food
Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose.
Definition: Photosynthesis - the conversion of light energy into chemical energy by chlorophyll-containing organisms.
The overall equation is: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2.
It happens inside chloroplasts, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll.
Light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes and produce ATP and NADPH.
Tip: Remember that the light reactions need water and light, and they release oxygen as a by-product.
The Calvin cycle, also called light-independent reactions, occurs in the stroma and uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into glucose.
Factors affecting the rate of photosynthesis include light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature and water availability.
Note: Excessively high temperatures denature enzymes, which reduces the rate of photosynthesis.
Example: A leaf placed in darkness will stop releasing oxygen because the light reactions shut down.
Plants use glucose for respiration, to build cellulose cell walls, and to store energy as starch.
Key Equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light -> C6H12O6 + 6O2 (occurs in the chloroplast)
